## Evidence and scope

- Magnitude: [Parkinson's Foundation statistics](https://www.parkinson.org/understanding-parkinsons/statistics) reports an estimated worldwide population above ten million. Preserve the estimated status in the source line. The page does not provide a reference year for this global bullet; do not label it a 2026 census or equate it with the earlier WHO 2019 estimate.
- Projection: [BMJ modelling study, 5 March 2025](https://www.bmj.com/content/388/bmj-2024-080952) projects 25.2 million people in 2050, with a 95% uncertainty interval of 21.7–30.1 million, using GBD 2021 data. This is a model projection, not a certain future count. The study's reported growth comparison uses its 2021 baseline, not the Foundation's above-ten-million estimate. Do not animate the two sources as one measured time series or derive a growth percentage from them.
- Fastest-growing: the BMJ paper's introduction attributes the ranking in prevalence and disability growth to GBD 2015. “Fastest-growing neurological condition” compresses this metric and historical scope. Prefer the numerical 2050 projection for the opening; retain the superlative as a shortlisted alternative, not an independently verified current ranking across every metric.
- Detection: [Parkinson's UK diagnosis explainer](https://www.parkinsons.org.uk/information/about-parkinsons/how-diagnosed) describes gradual symptom development, symptoms taking time to become obvious enough to seek medical advice, and variable diagnostic journeys. The preferred line is a qualitative summary, not a measured Lithuanian diagnostic delay. Consider specifying “recognised as Parkinson’s” to clarify what recognition means. It does not establish that VoxWave detects earlier.
- Access: [LRT-commissioned Lithuanian survey, May 2025](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/sveikata/682/2550683/eileje-pas-gydytojus-specialistus-daugumai-tenka-laukti-2-3-savaites-nerima-kelia-vyrai) reports 20% of respondents waited two to three months for a specialist; the most common category was two to four weeks (32%). The sample was 1,015 respondents across different settlements. This supports “can mean months”, not a typical wait of months for everyone. It is neither neurologist-specific nor rural-only.
