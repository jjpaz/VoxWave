# VoxWave — Cinematic opening, technical script v0.2

Updated: 2026-10-09. Working proposal. This document supersedes the opening in technical-script-v01.md.

## Confirmed direction

- The owner's contribution is the cinematic opening only, approximately 40–50 seconds maximum, including the handoff to the animated section.
- Never show the older person's face, including reflections, photographs or reverse angles.
- Use 2–4 cinematic shots, alternating with black screens, white messages and an orange waveform.
- The messages are the main focus. An everyday phone conversation with the person's daughter stays in the background, with a telephone filter, and drives the waveform.
- Explore solitude, distance and a rural setting through images. Additional shots and exact messages remain undecided.
- All audience-facing text and speech must be in English.
- Finish with the waveform alone on black, transforming into the official VoxWave logo on white.
- Evidence-led message sequence confirmed: magnitude of Parkinson's, delayed detection/diagnosis, and access to care. Both detection and access matter. Follow the three factual cards with a brief question about voice before the logo; its exact wording remains proposed.
- Owner-defined geographical market scope: TAM worldwide; SAM the Baltic states; SOM Lithuania. These are geographical scopes, not calculated market sizes or evidence of reachable demand.

## Recommended approach

Use three shots, three factual cards and a short closing question over 45 seconds. Progress from a small detail to a room and then to the landscape: hand → home → distance. This gives each return to live action a different purpose and a wider sense of space. Seek worldwide magnitude data and geographically relevant detection/access evidence, prioritising Lithuania and the Baltic states where available. Do not transfer findings from another population without identifying their scope.

The daughter calling already suggests a relationship. Aim for physical solitude and geographical distance, rather than implying that she has abandoned her parent. A quiet, inhabited home will communicate this more naturally than a ruined house, overt distress or staged neglect.

Keep the dialogue ordinary and low in the mix. Do not have the daughter explain symptoms, healthcare access or the product. No foreground narrator in this opening. It should remain understandable with the dialogue muted.

## Shot alternatives

| Option | What it communicates | How to keep it restrained |
|---|---|---|
| One cup and an empty chair | Physical solitude within an inhabited home | Keep the room cared for; avoid memorial photographs, medicine piles or an exaggerated abandoned setting. |
| Empty hallway, phone audible from another room | Quiet and separation within the house | Use depth and a partly open door; no face or silhouette reflection. |
| Rural house beyond a gate or long lane | Geographical distance | Use the same house as the interior where possible. A locked wide shot is sufficient; a drone is unnecessary. |
| Empty rural bus stop | Distance and mobility as visual motifs | Optional alternative to the house; avoid fabricated timetables or suggesting a specific transport/service deficit without evidence. |
| Rear view of a seated figure by a window | Solitude with a human presence | Optional fourth shot only; frame fully from behind and never reveal the profile. Not required for the recommended three-shot cut. |

Recommended combination: phone + cup/empty chair + rural exterior. It is visually varied, achievable and keeps the face entirely absent. If a fourth shot is chosen, replace part of the existing live-action time rather than adding another message cycle.

## Background dialogue — English scratch script

This is optional atmosphere, not a scene the audience must follow. Record with relaxed pauses; fit to approximately 35–38 seconds after the ring. The daughter's relationship is established in her first line. Use an authorized performance or fictional synthetic voices; never a private or clinical recording.

Older parent: “Hello?”

Daughter: “Hi, Dad. It's me. Just calling to see how your morning's going.”

Parent: “Oh, fine. I've just put the kettle on.”

Daughter: “Sounds nice. It's raining here again.”

Parent: “Still dry here, for now.”

Daughter: “Did you finish that book?”

Parent: “Nearly. A few pages left.”

“Dad” is a provisional casting choice; change to “Mum” if appropriate. No illness exposition, emotional climax or farewell is necessary. Let the call fade naturally as the waveform takes over.

## Typography, waveform and sound hierarchy

1. White messages are the primary focus. Use one readable message per card; avoid moving or typing text once visible.
2. The orange waveform is secondary. Use official coral #FE653C, a slim band and moderate amplitude; do not let peaks cross the text.
3. The call is a quiet background layer. Keep words recognizable when attended to, but do not mix it like narration. Preserve a clean recording before filtering.
4. Use dialogue amplitude before the final background-volume reduction to control the waveform. This keeps motion visible while the audible conversation stays low. Smooth the envelope and let it settle during pauses; do not invent constant speech activity.
5. Do not burn large dialogue subtitles over the message cards. If captions are required, plan a separate caption track and reserved lower area so the main messages remain legible.
6. Avoid a sombre music cue that overwhelms the domestic atmosphere. A restrained bed is optional; the strongest musical change belongs at the logo reveal.

## Required evidence-led copy

The owner selected magnitude, detection and access as the three factual messages. Research primary sources, population, year and scope before writing final copy; include a legible source. Diagnostic delay and the interval between biological changes and symptoms are different claims and must not be substituted for each other. Do not invent a rural figure if local evidence is unavailable. No clinical or rural-access statistics have been selected or verified for this draft. Placeholders must be replaced before any audience-facing export.

Do not use claims such as “Your voice reveals Parkinson's”, “We detect it before symptoms” or “A call can diagnose it”. Do not present worldwide prevalence as a calculated commercial TAM. Market labels need not appear in the cinematic opening.

## Production and handoff

- Build a 45-second animatic first using three still placeholders, the message cards and an English scratch call. Test it muted: the texts and images should convey the premise without the dialogue.
- Film three short takes or generate fictional shots from a shared visual reference. Keep sleeve, hand, phone, table, house, light and colour consistent. Generated hands touching phones need an early continuity test. No provider is selected by this document.
- Produce text, waveform and logo transition separately in post-production; do not ask an image/video generator to draw the final lettering or official logo.
- Keep cinematography quiet: locked frames or very slow movement. Low saturation and soft daylight, with readable shadows, rather than total darkness or simulated destitution.
- Reuse the current audiovisual project's 1920 × 1080 / 30 fps composition as a working format, subject to delivery requirements. This is a video production proposal, not a platform stack decision.
- Deliver a draft opening plus clean dialogue, filtered dialogue, ambience/music stems, shot list and final text. Confirm resolution, frame rate and whether the next editor needs a clean logo hold before final export.
- Agree that 00:45 is the opening's proposed handoff point, including the morph. Avoid duplicating the reveal in the subsequent section. Marketing, finance, product explanation and team presentation are outside this contribution.
- Keep raw media and renders in the existing local media locations; do not broaden Git audio/video exceptions. Review rights before external generation or publication.
